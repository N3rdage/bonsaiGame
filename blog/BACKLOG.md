## Math sub-series

A recurring sub-series on the geometry powering the visible game. Posts are tagged `[Math]` in the title, with `-math-` in the slug, for filtering. Pitched at high-school / early-uni readers — assume rough familiarity with `cos`/`sin` and don't be scared of an angle, but otherwise don't presume background.

**Convention:** keep at least the next 2–3 posts outlined here so each post can end with a teaser pointing at the next, and so we're always building toward a destination rather than picking ad hoc. When a post ships, drop it from this list and add a new candidate at the end to keep the buffer.

In sequence:

1. **[Math] Where does branch 3 actually start?** — the trunk-surface offset, the `branch_point(tree, branch, t)` helper. The trunk-attachment maths skipped over by both the trunk-bending and branch-curvature posts: a branch's attachment angle expressed in the trunk's local basis (now that the trunk has one), plus the trunk's radius at the branch's height. Bridges from "circle in the abstract" to "circle as the cross-section of a real game object whose orientation can change." Teased at the end of the branch-curvature AND the "3D viewer is mostly arithmetic" posts — next up.
2. **[Math] Wrapping wire round a branch** — local frames perpendicular to a branch direction; helix parametrisation; oriented rings via a cross-product basis. The maths from the wire-coil PR (#19), now updated for the curving branch. Linear algebra starts to earn its keep.
3. **[Math] Z-up in a y-down world (deeper cut)** — projection matrix sign flips, lookat conventions, why GameMaker's 3D tutorials get this subtly wrong for non-toy scenes. Partial overlap with the upside-down-tree post; this version goes further into the linear algebra. Topical hook: the trunk-bending UX shipped with screen-relative direction buttons that turned out to be Left/Right-swapped because of exactly this — a worked example of the handedness flip biting in practice. (Teased again at the end of the "3D viewer is mostly arithmetic" post.)
4. **[Math] What scoring 100 actually means** — the scoring function as a maths object. Weighted average of bounded criteria, vitality multiplier, style conformance as a plug-in. Less geometry, more "where does the number on the inspector come from." Lands well after the geometric posts have built up reader confidence.
5. **[Math] When the loop disappears** — closed-form vs numerical integration as a recurring craft choice. Trunk needed a walk; branch admitted a closed form; same family of curve, different problem geometry, different solution shape. Reflective rather than worked-example. Could land any time as a "let's pause and look at what we've learned" entry.
6. **[Math] What a 4×4 matrix actually does to a point** — the view-projection matrix unpacked, one column at a time. Picks up the thread from "the screen is a stack of coordinate systems" (which wrote the matrix-multiply out by hand without explaining it): what each of the 16 numbers contributes, why translation needs the 4th row, and what the perspective divide is really dividing by. The linear-algebra backbone under the projection post.
7. **[Math] Storing a camera as three angles** — spherical coordinates and the orbit camera. Why the viewer keeps yaw/pitch/distance instead of an (x,y,z) eye position, how `dcos`/`dsin` turn three angles into a position on a sphere, the `cos(pitch)` radius-shrink that tucks the camera overhead, and gimbal-ish edge cases at the poles. Teased in the "3D viewer is mostly arithmetic" post (Stop 1).
8. **[Math] From a point on a branch to a dot you can tap** — `branchPoint` gives a world position in viewer units; `projectToScreen` in `src/viewer/projection.ts` turns it into CSS pixels (the view matrix, the perspective divide, the `(ndc + 1) / 2` remap, the y flip, and why a point behind the camera returns nothing). Then why the hit test happens in pixels, not metres: a dot of 8 px radius with a 16 px hit radius is the same size on a phone whatever the zoom, so a far branch is as tappable as a near one. The hotspot overlay as the place where the game's real units and the screen's pixels finally meet.
9. **[Math] A pen line that stays 2 pixels wide** — the ink hull's vertex shader in `src/viewer/TreeViewer.ts` (`OUTLINE_VERTEX`). Each vertex is set back `OUTLINE_DEPTH` (0.1 display units) along the line of sight in view space, projected to clip space, then pushed along its normal's on-screen direction by `uWidth * clip.w`. Why multiplying by `w` is the whole trick: the perspective divide then cancels it, so the push is a fixed amount in normalised device coordinates however far away the vertex is. And why `uWidth` is `2 * OUTLINE_WIDTH_PX / width`: NDC spans 2 units across the viewport, so that is 2 CSS px at any zoom or pixel ratio. A follow-on to item 6's perspective divide, with a working example of using it on purpose.
10. **[Math] Scattering tiles without a random number** — `floorFrame` in `src/world/assets.ts`, `(column * 7 + row * 13) mod frames`, which picks each floor tile's variant and now the shed's wall face and caps too. Why a stable hash of the tile's position beats a random pick (the room looks the same every bake and every reload, nothing flickers or animates, and a test can pin it), and why with 7 and 13 against 3 frames each step of one column or one row changes the frame by 1, so neighbours differ by construction. Then what repeats if the multipliers are chosen badly: a multiplier that is a multiple of the frame count makes whole columns or rows identical, and multipliers equal modulo the frame count give `(column + row) mod frames`, diagonal stripes, which is honestly what 7 and 13 reduce to at 3 frames (both are 1 mod 3), so the post should look at whether the eye catches it.
