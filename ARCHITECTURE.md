# Architecture

How the web project is organised, the decisions behind it, and the invariants carried over from the GameMaker prototype. For why the rewrite happened, see `docs/ADR-001-typescript-rewrite.md`. For the prototype's own architecture, see `archive/gamemaker/ARCHITECTURE.md`. For deliberately-unsimulated behaviour, see `SIMPLIFICATIONS.md`.

The web build is at parity with the prototype: it has the simulation and the 3D viewer; the title scene with new game, continue, the slot picker and F5/F9 save and load; the day clock, and the shed and the garden drawn from the prototype's sprites and joined by doors, with the garden's source plants giving cuttings, the shed's kiosk selling supplies, its workbench making pots and its planting table planting cuttings; player movement, the HUD, interactables, modal panels, tree sprites and carrying, and the tree inspector with its sub-panels; the tutorial corner panel, its Skip confirmation and Granny's notebook; per-machine settings with the Settings panel; seasonal ambient weather in the garden and dust motes in the shed; and every text in Caveat, a hand-lettered script. `e2e/parity.spec.ts` plays that loop end to end in one browser test in landscape, walking on the arrow keys and taking every action by tap, in its own Playwright project (`s22-landscape-parity`) that runs after the four per-step projects: beside them, its 3D viewer render on CI's software GL starved the other worker's walks past their timeouts. What the port deliberately leaves out is noted in the porting map's rows and under "Known rough edges".

## High-level shape

A single-page web game. Vite bundles TypeScript into a static site; Phaser renders the 2D world and UI to a canvas; Three.js renders the 3D tree viewer (pot, trunk, branch, foliage and wire meshes, hotspots, training actions and the pause-while-open lifecycle). The simulation is a set of pure TypeScript modules with no rendering dependency, so it runs identically in the browser, in unit tests, and in any future headless tool.

```
index.html
  src/main.ts            Phaser.Game config, PWA service-worker registration
  src/scenes/            Phaser scenes: boot, title, world (every room)
  src/world/             pure room data (the shed, the back and front gardens, the house rooms, the greenhouse), player movement, input and HUD text for the world scene; resolution.ts, the canvas and camera scale
  src/sim/               pure simulation: tree, geometry, growth, seasons, scoring, species, styles, training, save
  src/world3d/           Babylon.js 3D world: plan.ts (a room's 3D plan in metres, derived from src/world/), meshes.ts, character.ts, trees.ts and treeMeshes.ts are pure, renderer.ts draws with Babylon, world3d.ts is the world scene's adapter, bench.ts (pure) is the Menu's on-screen frame-time benchmark and benchPanel.ts shows its result (and, from the Menu's Room times row, the latest room-change and build-ahead times that roomTimes.ts words), paint/ (pure) paints the look's surface textures (wallpaper, wood, tiles) as byte rasters with seeded brush strokes, no canvas, and `PaintCache` paints each once per session
  src/viewer/            Three.js tree viewer: scaffold, pot, trunk/branch/foliage/wire meshes, hotspots and modes, toolbar, actions, lifecycle
  e2e/                   Playwright browser tests
  public/                static assets, PWA icons, staticwebapp.config.json
```

The logical resolution is 480x270, fitted to the window with crisp text over pixel art (see the resolution bullet under "How the scenes are shaped"). This replaces the prototype's room-sized viewports.

## Module rules

- **`src/sim/` imports nothing from Phaser, Three or the DOM.** It takes plain data in and returns plain data out. This is what makes the game testable without a browser and is the main thing the rewrite buys.
- **`src/world3d/` is derived from `src/world/`, never authored beside it.** `roomPlan3d` turns a `Room` into axis-aligned boxes and anchor points in metres (x east, y south, z up, 32 room px to the metre): the floor, the walls extruded to their height with door and window openings cut, the furniture over its footprint (a wall's cap is no box of its own, the wall it caps is one, and a window in a building's face is a detail set in it), a `Roof` over each building (a pitched gable on the back garden's, one slope for the front garden's strip, drawn as a prism by `roofMeshes.ts` and cut away with the pieces), the shed's workbench, planting table and kiosk as models over their footprints and its rug flat on the floor, and every prop as an anchor. The 2D room data stays the single source of truth for layout, and movement and collision stay on the 2D plan: the plan's solid footprints plus its door openings equal the 2D collision rects exactly, which a unit test pins for every room. `plan.ts` and `meshes.ts` are pure, importing no Phaser, Three, Babylon or DOM, and are unit-tested. `renderer.ts` is the Babylon.js side and is reached only through a dynamic `import()` from `world3d.ts`, the world scene's adapter, so Babylon sits in its own chunk and loads only while the 3D world setting is on. The camera is an `ArcRotateCamera` driven from our own code, never Babylon's `attachControl` (the Babylon canvas takes no pointer events). The pure `camera.ts` holds the session's `CameraRig` (heading alpha, pitch beta, distance radius; module state that survives room changes and is never saved), the clamps as named constants (`BETA_MIN`/`BETA_MAX`, `RADIUS_MIN`/`RADIUS_MAX`, `RADIUS_DEFAULT`), `CameraGesture` (one pointer is the stick or turns, two are a pinch that only zooms; the second finger cancels what the first began, wherever each lands, and the pinch lasts until every finger is up; the game config gives Phaser two touch pointers, which a pinch needs) and the cut-away rule: indoors `facesCamera(side, alpha)` lowers each wall on the camera's side to `SKIRTING_HEIGHT` (two sides at a heading between them), and outdoors (garden, front garden) `cutNames` lowers only the tall pieces the line from the camera to the player passes through. The renderer eases the camera and its look-at point (the player) toward the rig each frame and publishes `camera`, `cut` and `facing` on the test hook. Walking is read against the camera: while the 3D world is on, the scene turns the key or stick vector by the rig's heading with the pure `cameraRelative` (`move.ts`, a rotation, so the speed is unchanged and the default heading is the identity) before the unchanged 2D `stepPlayer` and its collision run, so up is always away from the camera; the rig's target heading is used rather than the eased one, so a turn redirects a held key at once and a test can predict it. The player is a rounded, skinned character built in code, not loaded: `character.ts` (pure) holds seven joints (hips, spine, head, two legs, two arms) with their parents and rest offsets, 38 ellipsoid parts each weighted wholly to one joint (`characterMesh`, 1.7 to 1.75 m tall with its feet on y 0, facing +z; the painterly gardener, with a big round head, a knotted headscarf and bun, rolled sleeves, an apron with a trowel and boots, its colours in `GARDENER_PALETTE`, painted into the vertex colours with a top light and a seeded brush wander), the `idle` (3 s) and `walk` (1 s) clips as keyed rotations (and the hips' bob), and the blend: `targetWeights(speed)` is idle 1 at rest and walk rising linearly to 1 at `WALK_FULL_SPEED` (1 m/s), `fadeWeights` eases the walk weight to it over 0.2 s so a change cross-fades, `walkRate` plays the walk at speed over `STRIDE_SPEED` (the speed at which the feet keep pace with the ground), and `poseOf` mixes the clips by weight into each joint's local rotation and translation. The renderer measures the player's speed from the metres its feet moved over the frame's seconds, smoothed over 0.1 s, builds one `Skeleton` of seven `Bone`s and one skinned mesh from `characterMesh`, and each frame sets each bone's local matrix from `poseOf`; no glTF and no loader are involved, so nothing loads asynchronously. The body turns to the heading of its last step (`facingOf`, published as `facing`, radians in the plan, 0 east) and keeps it at rest; when the browser prefers reduced motion the idle clip holds its first pose, so a player at rest is still (the e2e suite sets it, which keeps screenshots deterministic); the hook's `world3d.playerModel` is `'character'` and `world3d.clips` the idle and walk weights. In the world scene Q and E turn the camera in 45 degree steps (E only when nothing is in reach to interact with, Q only when no panel is open), the wheel and a pinch zoom, and a press on bare ground turns the camera, except a touch on the left half of the screen, which is the movement stick (`pressRoute`); a press on a HUD button or panel never reaches the camera. Taps work as in 2D by walking and interacting: the pure `tap.ts` holds `TapTracker` (a press the camera took is a tap only if, over its whole life, it never moved more than `TAP_MAX_MOVE_PX` game px, was released within `TAP_MAX_MS` and no second pointer joined, so a drag that turned the camera never is one) and `routeInput` (the straight plan-space step toward a point, not turned by the camera). A tap calls `Renderer.pick`, which uses Babylon's `scene.pick` with the CSS-px point on the Babylon canvas (the same rect as the game's): first against the invisible hotspot boxes `hotspotSpecs` builds from the plan's anchors (one per prop, so the id is the prop's own `key`, as `near` and the interact prompt use), widened for a finger: `tap.ts`'s pure `nearestSpot` takes the prop whose projected middle is nearest the tap within a radius, or the prop the ray hit if that is nearer, and the scene passes the radius `ui.hit` game px (one 44 CSS px touch target) for a touch tap and 0 for the mouse, which must hit the box; a prop behind the camera (projected depth outside 0 to 1) is never picked; else against the floor, whose hit `roomPx` (the inverse of `toBabylon` and `playerFeet`) converts back to room px. The scene turns a hotspot into a goal at the prop and a floor hit into a goal at the point; each fixed step the goal's `routeInput` replaces the keys until the prop is the scene's `nearestInteractable` (then `interact()` runs, as the Interact button does), the point is reached, a key or the stick is pressed, a panel opens, the room changes or a step goes nowhere (there is no path-finding). Doors in the 3D world are walked through, not interacted with: after each step `doorWalkedInto` (`doorWalk.ts`, pure) says whether the player stands in a door's trigger strip (across its width and within `DOOR_REACH`, its Interact range, of its centre on the room side) while moving toward it (a motion at least `DOOR_APPROACH_COS` along the way into the door, so walking along its wall never takes it), and the scene then runs the door's ordinary `enterRoom`, so the landing is the one Interact would give; a landing is further out than `DOOR_REACH`, which a unit test pins for every door, so arriving never re-triggers. `DOOR_RULES.walkThrough` turns it off and leaves the doors to Interact and the tap walk; the 2D world never walks through doors. While the 3D world draws, the prompt is a HUD text above the Interact button, since the world camera's label is not seen. The hook's `world3d` carries `picked` (the last hotspot id) and `hotspots` (each id's projected screen point in game px). The player's trees stand where the 2D world draws them and are the tree viewer's own meshes, not a second tree model: `treeStands` (`trees.ts`) takes `treeSprites` for the room (so the floor grid, its wrap and the displayed trees are the 2D world's), converts room px to plan metres and stands a displayed tree in the middle of its spot on top of the furniture under it (`stands.height` through `heightMetres`); `treeMeshData` (`treeMeshes.ts`) runs `buildPotGeometry`, `buildTreeMesh` and `buildFoliageMesh`, and `worldMeshData` puts their arrays in Babylon's frame (display units divided by `DISPLAY_SCALE` to metres, y and z swapped, which keeps the viewer's handedness, RGBA vertex colours, both windings as the viewer draws), so the pot, in its tier's palette, and the tree are at real scale. `meshOf` in `renderer.ts` hands those arrays to Babylon's `VertexData`, the only tree code that touches Babylon. A tree's meshes are rebuilt only when its `treeKey` (id, `revision`, trunk height, foliage density, pot tier and the season) changes; each frame only the stand's position is set. A tree that left the room is disposed, and a room change disposes the room's trees. Each display spot with no tree wears a mat: `spotMats` (`trees.ts`) gives one per empty spot, at the stand a tree there would have, and the renderer lays and disposes them by spot key the way it does trees, each a low inked stone stand tall enough to rise clear of the furniture's own outline shell. The hook's `world3d.spotMats` counts them. A room's boxes, details and art are built once and kept, not rebuilt each visit, and the rooms behind the shown room's doors (`neighbourKeys`, from the live room data the scene gives, placed furniture included) are built hidden ahead of the change, one a frame, only once the player has stood still for 200 ms and the shown room is dressed (a build is one blocking step, which a walk would feel), so a door usually leads to a built room; every floor and wall sheet starts loading when the renderer starts. A set hangs from one `TransformNode` per room, which a room change hides (the shadow map skips a disabled caster) and shows again with every piece back at full height and the shadow frustum refitted, and `roomSignature` (`roomCache.ts`, pure, with the capped log of room changes) says when the live room's furniture or props changed since the set was built, which builds it afresh. The hook's `world3d.built` lists the keys of the sets kept, `world3d.aheadBuilds` logs each room built ahead with the ms its build took, and `world3d.roomChanges` lists the latest changes of room with whether each built the room's set (`first`) and the ms from the start of its frame to the end of its render, plus `dressedMs` for a built set's art, and `meshes` counts only what is shown. The hook's `world3d.trees` counts the standing trees. Furniture with a model (the player's pedestals and floor shelves, the shed's shelf, tools, watering can, pot stack and sack) is drawn as that model, not as the plan's box: `furnitureModels.ts` holds each kind's few boxes in metres with palette washes (`MODELLED_SHEETS` says which sheets stand for which kind), and `furniturePieces` (`furniture.ts`) lists a room's modelled pieces from the same `furnitureBox` the plan uses, as footprint middle, size, height and rotation (always 0, as the 2D world has no turned furniture) with a `key` that changes exactly when the piece's instance must. The scene hands the renderer the live room (its own furniture and props plus the placed items), so a placed item is a piece and its display spots are hotspots. Each kind is one merged, vertex-coloured source mesh drawn with thin instances, scaled from its nominal size to the piece's footprint and height, so a piece's top is the height `surfaceAt` stands trees at. Each frame the renderer compares each kind's piece keys and the props' keys with the last frame's: placing, moving or picking up an item resets that kind's instance buffer (a kind with none left is hidden, its source mesh kept for the next room that has it) and remakes the hotspots, and the room's boxes are never rebuilt. The hook's `world3d.instances` counts the instances of each kind. The world has the game's one light, never a second: `light.ts` turns `CAST_SHADOW` into the directional light's direction in plan metres (a ray that drops 1 runs `CAST_SHADOW.length` along the 2D direction, so a shadow falls down and to the right with the 2D length per unit of height) and `SHADOW_ALPHA` into `ShadowGenerator.darkness` (its complement). One plain `ShadowGenerator` serves, not cascades, since one room at a time is small and always wholly in view; the player, each tree part and each furniture kind's source mesh cast, and only the floor receives. The world's look is painterly, and its numbers are the data of the pure `look.ts` (`LOOK`; no Babylon import, unit-tested): a soft three-level light ramp over N.L, a warm sun, a hemisphere fill (a blue-grey sky above, a plum bounce below, so a shadow takes a hue rather than grey), a thin ink for the silhouettes, and the image processing's exposure and contrast. `look.ts` also derives the shader text (`rampGlsl`), the light's colours as 0..1 triples (`lookLight`) and `outlineWidthMetres` (the outline's width in metres for a width in px at the default camera radius, the camera's field of view and the canvas's height). Every lit mesh (floor, walls, roofs, furniture boxes and models, trees, the player) is lit by `lookPlugin.ts`, a `MaterialPluginBase` on each StandardMaterial that replaces the lit colour's last step with the base colour (palette wash, texture or vertex colour) times the fill plus the sun through the ramp: `fill = mix(ground, sky, n.y * 0.5 + 0.5) * mix(0.75, 1.0, shadow)` and `sun * ramp(dot(n, toLight)) * shadow`, with the signed N.L, so cast shadows still darken by `SHADOW_ALPHA` and dim the fill a little (the one sun's shadow map has a frustum fitted once per room by the pure `shadowFrustum`, not refitted to the casters, with a depth bias and a normal bias) and vertex colours and thin instances work as before. The plugin reads the look's constants directly, since Babylon's base constructor asks for the shader code before a subclass's fields exist. The renderer sets the scene's image processing (exposure and contrast) from `LOOK`; there is no vignette, which would darken the edges of the 3D view's own box rather than the screen's. The player, the tree parts and the furniture (boxes and each model's thin-instanced mesh) carry Babylon's built-in outline in `LOOK.ink`, which draws a thin-instanced mesh in the same one call; walls, floor and the weather do not. The renderer sets the outline width again whenever the canvas's size changes. The season's weather is the 2D world's own field, not a second model: the scene steps `createParticles`/`stepParticles` for the room as ever and hands the field to the renderer outdoors only (indoors the 3D world draws none, and the weather setting turns it off as in 2D); `weatherSprites` (`weather.ts`) lays each particle from `particleDraw` in the plan at 32 px to the metre, the field's fall as height under a 4 m ceiling and its depth spread evenly by particle index, and the renderer draws them as one thin-instanced unlit quad turned to the camera's heading, tinted by instance colour. The hook's `world3d.shadows` says whether anything casts and `world3d.weather` is the kind drawn or null. The floor wears the 2D floor's Inked tiles: `floorLayout` (`floorTiles.ts`) turns `floorTiles(room)` (the sheet, the area and the frame per tile, never re-hashed) into one quad per tile in plan metres with each frame's UV rect, the frames read left to right then down the sheet's image as Phaser slices it, inset half a texel against bleeding, and `floorMeshData` into one mesh's vertex arrays, 3 mm above the floor box's top. The renderer loads the same PNG Phaser does as a mipmapped, anisotropic, clamped `Texture` (a pixel-art sheet magnifies nearest, so shrunk art averages instead of crawling), and builds the mesh once it has loaded, as a look-lit shadow-receiving StandardMaterial with no outline; the floor box stays underneath as the dark surround outside an inset area and as the tap's floor. A texture that fails to load is disposed and tried again by the next room that wants it. The hook's `world3d.floorSheet` is the sheet key in use, null until it has loaded. The walls wear the 2D room's Inked art the same way: `wallLayout` (`wallTiles.ts`) lays each run of `wallRuns` (minus the windows, which the plan cuts as openings) on the plan's wall boxes in metres with each frame's UV rect inset half a texel. A back wall's face goes on the north wall's inner face, the full wall height; the other walls' caps on their tops (only on a piece that reaches the wall's height); and a fence's bands (rows, columns and corner posts) on both long faces, the frame spanning the fence's height. A run is laid on the pieces of the first wall that claims it, so a corner tile is drawn once, and each piece shows the part of the run it covers, so a window's sill and lintel pieces carry their part of the face. Art sits `WALL_LIFT` off the box's surface. The renderer builds, for each piece the camera can cut (`cuttable` in `camera.ts`: any wall indoors, only tall pieces outdoors), one mesh per sheet parented to the piece's box, so a cut-away, which scales and lowers the box, takes its art with it (one mesh per sheet over several pieces could not follow their separate cuts); the pieces that always stand, a garden's fences, share one merged mesh per sheet. A papered mass (the lounge's hallway block, `spr_paper`) is furniture washed in its room's wall wash, and `massLayout` dresses it as a wall: the room's face tiles on its south and west faces, the faces a player sees, and its cap runs on its top, in the same merged mesh, since indoors only walls are cut. A sheet's meshes share one material, mipmapped, look lit, with no outline and no shadows, as the boxes are; the art materials carry a polygon offset toward the camera (`ART_DEPTH_OFFSET`), so the 3 mm lift never has to win a depth test alone. Both floor and walls load through one path: each sheet's texture is made once per renderer, shared by every room that wears it and disposed with the renderer, and one arriving after its room's set was disposed is not laid on it. The hook's `world3d.wallSheets` lists the loaded wall sheets' keys, sorted. The rooms' details are modelled, not textured, because the plan's door and window openings are real holes that the Inked sheets, drawn flat on a face, cannot fill: `roomDetails` (`details.ts`, pure) gives each plan opening a door (a frame round a leaf) or a window (a frame round a pane, with a mullion when wide), each door prop against a building's face (which cuts no opening) a recessed leaf in a frame hung from that face (a door is as wide as its sprite, its prop or a metre, whichever is most, `doorWidth` in `plan.ts`, for the cut and the frame alike; a glazed one, `isGlazedDoor`, has a translucent unoutlined pane of glass in wood rails in place of planks), each flat `spr_bed` furniture piece a box hedge and each source plant a stacked-box crown of its own species (a juniper cone, a round maple, a layered pine) over its 0.75 m footprint, as boxes in metres with palette washes. The renderer merges the boxes of each wall piece's details into one vertex-coloured mesh parented to that piece's lintel (else its sill), so the cut-away takes them along, lit like the walls and not outlined (a detail on a building face is inked, as the face is, or the face's outline would paint over it; a pane of glass is a child mesh on its own translucent material, and a piece's details are hidden while the camera has lowered it); the hedges and plantings are one merged mesh on the floor, a shadow caster and inked like furniture. The hook's `world3d.details` counts the room's details by kind.
- **Scenes own rendering and input, never game rules.** A scene calls into `src/sim/` and draws the result. If a rule needs a number, the number lives in `src/sim/`.
- **Test hook.** Scenes publish a minimal read-only snapshot to `window.__bonsai` (type `BonsaiTestHook` in `src/vite-env.d.ts`). Browser tests assert on it instead of inspecting canvas pixels. It is additive: add fields, never repurpose them.

### How the simulation is shaped

- `src/sim/types.ts` is the whole data model: plain interfaces, JSON-serialisable, no methods. `BonsaiTree.revision` replaces the prototype's `mesh_dirty` flag; every morphology mutation goes through `markDirty` or `addBranch`, and renderers rebuild when the number changes.
- `GameState` carries what the prototype kept in globals (day, money, inventory, trees, next id, tutorial step), plus the placed furniture. Simulation functions take it explicitly and mutate it in place; that is what makes them testable.
- Randomness is injected: `treeDailyTick(tree, state, isolated, rng)` and `advanceDayAllTrees(state, days, rng)` default to `Math.random`, tests pass a constant.
- The prototype's numbers are canon unless a backlog decision says otherwise. Departures: the wire safe-bend limit in `training.ts` reads branch girth in centimetres (`60 - (girth / 10) * 20`), where the GML applied the same constants to millimetres, leaving the starter's branches limits of 6 degrees or less (negative above 3 mm) so the penalty was either instant or could never fire. Pruning also renumbers wire and clip history ids, which the prototype left pointing at the wrong branch. Taking a cutting is limited to spring and summer, and pruning to winter; the prototype allowed both all year. The viewer's wire coil rides the floored branch bark radius, where the prototype's `add_wire_coil` followed the unfloored taper and sank the wire into the bark near a thin branch's tip.
- Saves are versioned. `save.ts` writes version 7 (camelCase; version 4's shape plus the placed furniture, `placed` and `nextPlacedId`, and the time of day, `timeOfDayS`) and reads version 7 (a time of day that is not a number or lies outside [0, `SECONDS_PER_DAY`) loads as morning, 0), version 6 (version 7's shape without the time of day; it and every older version wake at morning, `timeOfDayS: 0`), version 5 (version 6's shape, laid out on the old 22x10-tile greenhouse: it passes through `migrateGreenhouseGrid`, which keeps every greenhouse item still wholly on the 10x7 floor, `GREENHOUSE_FLOOR` in `src/sim/greenhouseFloor.ts`, at its x, y, returns any other to the inventory and puts a tree displayed on one of its spots down on the greenhouse floor, `greenhouse`, leaving `nextPlacedId` alone; a version-6 save is never moved), version 4 (version 3's shape with the display spots' final keys; it and every older version load with no placed furniture, `placed: []` and `nextPlacedId: 0`), version 3 (version 2's fields plus `seasonTipShown`), version 2 (migrated with `seasonTipShown` false, so the first-dormancy tip can still fire once) and the prototype's version 1 through `importPrototypeSave` (also `seasonTipShown` false). Every save before version 4 passes its displayed trees through one table, `PRE_V4_DISPLAY_KEYS`, applied in `migrateDisplayKeys`: the shed's pedestals moved to the lounge (`shed_main` to `lounge_mantel`, `shed_window` to `lounge_side_table`; the prototype's saves carry these) and the house's short-lived version-3 keys took the names of the furniture they display on (`lounge_main` to `lounge_mantel`, `lounge_window` to `lounge_side_table`, `study_main` to `study_desk`, `study_window` to `study_bookshelf`). The plain renames go first, so in a version-3 save with trees on both `lounge_main` and `shed_main` the lounge's tree keeps the mantel and the shed's is put down on the shed floor (`shed`). The importer tolerates the trailing NUL byte the prototype wrote, GameMaker's float-typed integers, and the fields older saves lack. Every version is read as untrusted text: a `__proto__` key in a prototype save is never read as a field (the importer's key renaming skips it and builds prototype-less objects), arrays over the `MAX_SAVE_*` caps and a location or room key over 64 characters refuse the save as corrupt, and a tree name is cut to the 20-character rename cap. Two real prototype saves live in `src/sim/__fixtures__/` as importer tests, beside a hand-built version-5 save for the version 6 migration. Storage is behind a three-method `SaveStorage` interface with an in-memory implementation for tests and a `localStorage` one under `src/platform/`.

### How the scenes are shaped

- `BootScene` (`boot`) is the entry: it creates the one `LocalStorageSaveStorage` the game uses and the `LocalStorageSettingsStorage`, loads the `Settings` once, reads the viewer's `?tree=` and `?season=` test parameters, and starts `TitleScene` (`title`) with them. Every later scene receives the storages, the settings and those overrides through scene data (`src/scenes/sceneData.ts`), so a test could swap the storage; no scene creates its own. The settings are one mutable object shared by the scenes for the session.
- `TitleScene` (`title`, obj_title) shows the title, the version and four buttons: **New Game** (Enter) starts `createNewGameState()` in slot 1 when every slot is empty, otherwise opens the slot picker in "new" mode, where an occupied slot asks to confirm the overwrite (Y/N); **Continue** (C) loads `mostRecentSaveSlot` through `loadGame`, greyed out with no save; **Load Game** (L) opens the picker in "load" mode, where only slots with a save can be picked; **Settings** (S) opens the Settings panel (below) as a title view in the standard `PANEL` box, which Escape or Close returns from. Picker rows (keys 1-3, Escape to cancel) come from the pure `src/world/saveSlots.ts` (`readSlotRows`, the prototype's row text). An unreadable slot counts as occupied: the title says so on arrival, and loading it shows the error and stays on the title. Placeholder-styled like the world; the prototype's 3D hero tree is not ported, and its Quit is not ported at all: a web page has no quit, the browser closes the tab.
- **Phones run landscape only** (pure rule in `src/platform/orientation.ts`, wired in `src/main.ts`): a touch screen with a short side under 500 CSS px held in portrait gets the `#rotate-prompt` DOM overlay, which takes every tap while `game.pause()` stops the whole Phaser loop (day clock, world steps) and `game.input.enabled` is off (Phaser also listens on the window) until it is turned back, and on a phone the first tap on New Game, Continue or Load asks for fullscreen (the Settings toggle's `scale.startFullscreen()`) and, on entering it, a landscape lock, carrying on if refused; the manifest's `orientation` is `landscape`, and tablets and desktops are untouched. Portrait is not a supported layout because the logical canvas is a fixed 16:9 480x270, which on an upright phone fits a strip about a third of the screen tall with text too small to read and tap.
- **Settings** (scr_settings, obj_ui_settings): per-machine, separate from the save slots. The pure `src/platform/settings.ts` has `Settings { fullscreen, weather, sound, music, world3d, timeSpeed }`, `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` (`fullscreen: false`, `weather: true`, as `init_settings`, `sound: true`, `music: true`, and `timeSpeed: 1`, so settings stored before a switch existed load with its default), `loadSettings(storage)` (the defaults with only the known boolean keys, and a `timeSpeed` that is one of `TIME_SPEEDS`, from storage laid over them; unknown keys, wrong types, malformed JSON or an unreadable store leave the defaults) and `saveSettings`, behind a two-method `SettingsStorage` (`read`, `write`). `LocalStorageSettingsStorage` keeps the JSON under the one key `bonsai.settings`; reads are guarded like the save storage's, and a failed write is swallowed (a toggle must not throw out of a tap) so the value lives on in memory for the session. The prototype's window `scale` (1x-3x) is not ported: the canvas already fits the browser window (see Resolution). The **Settings panel** (pure model `settingsPanel(settings, { available, active })` in `src/world/settingsPanel.ts`, in `PANEL`): `Fullscreen: On|Off`, `Weather effects: On|Off`, `Sound: On|Off` (`panel-sound`), `Music: On|Off` (`panel-music`) and Close, which at `PHONE_UI` `fitRows` grows to a 176 px box; a toggle (`toggleSetting` in `src/scenes/settings.ts`) flips the value, applies it, saves and redraws the panel in place. **Fullscreen** calls Phaser's `scale.startFullscreen()` or `stopFullscreen()` inside the tap, the user gesture browsers require, and stores the intended value. The fullscreen element is the whole document (`fullscreenTarget: document.documentElement` in `src/main.ts`), not Phaser's default wrapper around the canvas, because the 3D viewer and the Rename input are DOM siblings of `#game` and would not be rendered outside the fullscreen subtree; `#game` fills the viewport either way, so the FIT refit on `resize` is unchanged. The row shows the live `scale.isFullscreen`, not the stored flag, and both scenes redraw an open panel on the scale manager's `ENTER_FULLSCREEN` and `LEAVE_FULLSCREEN`, so leaving with the browser's Escape shows at once. Nothing is applied at boot: browsers refuse fullscreen outside a gesture, so a stored `fullscreen: true` is not re-applied. Where `document.fullscreenEnabled` is not true (iOS Safari) the row reads `Fullscreen: not available`, grey and not a control. **Weather effects** gates the world's ambient weather (below). **Sound** and **Music** are the mixer, two on/off switches rather than volumes (the prototype had none): the pure `src/platform/audio.ts` has `audioAllowed(settings, kind)`, the one gate every play call goes through (`settings.sound` for `'sound'`, `settings.music` for `'music'`), the file keys `SOUND_KEYS` (`water`, `snip`, `wire`, `footstep`, `click`, `door`, `coin`) and `MUSIC_KEYS` (`shed`, `garden`, `house`, `viewer`), `trackFor(place)` (a room key or `'viewer'` to its loop: the lounge, hallway and study share `house`, and the greenhouse plays `garden`), `MUSIC_FADE_MS` (1200), `MUSIC_VOLUME` (0.4, under the effects' 1), and `audioUrl(base, key)`, `<base>assets/audio/<key>.wav` for every key, effects and music alike. **Sound effects** play through Phaser's own sound manager (Web Audio, which unlocks itself on the first pointer event; no audio config of our own) behind the thin adapter `SoundPlayer` (`src/scenes/audio.ts`): built on a `{ play(key) }` manager (`scene.sound`) and a settings getter, `play(key)` does nothing while `audioAllowed(settings, 'sound')` is false (read live, so the switch acts without a reload), else records `lastSound` and calls the manager inside a try/catch, so a browser that refuses audio never throws out of a tap; `tap(action)` runs a button's action and then plays `click` only if the action played nothing itself. `BootScene` loads all seven. What plays when: `click` on every Phaser button tap in the world (`hudButton`, which every HUD, menu and panel control goes through) and on the title's buttons, so turning Sound off is silent and turning it on clicks; `water` when the inspector's Water waters; `door` on every room change (`enterRoom`); `coin` on a confirmed sale and on a day whose display revenue is above 0; `footstep` on each fixed step whose walk cycle enters a contact frame, 0 or 2 (`footstepDue` in `src/world/player.ts`, twice per cycle); `snip` when a clip or prune changes the tree and `wire` when wiring a branch or the trunk, or the removal modal's Remove, does (the viewer's `onSound` callback, set by the world; a refusal, without wire or out of season, is silent). The files are short mono 16-bit 22050 Hz WAVs generated by `art/sounds.mjs` (`npm run art:sounds`, seeded, byte-identical on a rerun, all peak-normalised alike; 67784 bytes in all, listed in `public/assets/audio/README.md`): no Ogg encoder is available to the build, and WAV decodes in every target browser. Workbox precaches `wav`. **Music** is one ambient loop per place behind `MusicPlayer` (`src/scenes/audio.ts`), built on a `MusicManager` (`add(key, { loop, volume })` returning a sound with `play`, `stop`, `pause`, `resume`, `setVolume`, `destroy`; `scene.sound` satisfies it) and the same live settings getter, every manager call inside a try/catch. `play(key)` remembers the place's loop and, if `audioAllowed(settings, 'music')`, cross-fades to it: the new loop rises from 0 to `MUSIC_VOLUME` while every loop still sounding falls from where it is to 0 and is then stopped and destroyed, both over `MUSIC_FADE_MS`; the same key again is a no-op. `track` is the key playing after the gate, or ''. The fades advance in `update(delta)` from the world's frame loop. `refresh()` re-reads the switch: off stops every loop at once, on starts the remembered loop. The world plays `trackFor(room)` on create (a new or loaded game starts in the shed) and in `enterRoom`, `trackFor('viewer')` in `openViewer` and the room's again in the viewer's close handler, calls `refresh()` after the Settings panel's Music toggle, and `stop()` on shutdown, so the title (which has no player) is silent. Phaser's sound manager pauses on BLUR but not on `game.pause()`, which the rotate prompt uses (`src/main.ts`), so the world listens to the game's `PAUSE` and `RESUME` events and calls the player's `pause()` and `resume()`; `RESUME` also fires when a hidden page is shown again (`Game#onVisible`), which leaves `game.isPaused` set, so the world resumes the music only when the game is not paused. The loops are 8 s mono 16-bit 11025 Hz WAVs generated by `art/music.mjs` (`npm run art:music`, seeded and byte-identical on a rerun): slow sine chords under a soft LFO, warm in the shed, airy with a breeze and chirps in the garden, a music box in the house, plain and slow in the viewer; seamless because every frequency is a whole number of cycles per loop, chords and note tails wrap round, and the garden's noise is cross-faded end into start (705776 bytes in all).
- The world plays one `GameState` (new, or the one loaded) with an **active slot**. Save (the HUD menu or F5) saves it there (`saveGame`, captured so the browser does not reload) and shows `Saved to slot N` for 2 seconds; F9 reloads the slot and restarts the world with it. On an empty or unreadable active slot F9 opens the in-room **Load Game** picker instead, as the prototype's controller did (see the panels below); Close keeps the unsaved game. Escape with the viewer closed returns to the title without saving; there is no autosave. The world creates the `TreeViewer`, owns it until shutdown and publishes the test hook, whose `trees`, `starterScore` and `starterHeightCm` come from the state the world started with, and `day` and `season` from the state at each publish (the day clock moves them).
- **Day clock** (pure `src/world/clock.ts`, the controller's Step_0): `tickClock(state, deltaS, timeScale)` adds the frame's real seconds times the time scale (`effectiveTimeScale(override, speed)`: `base_time_scale`, 1, or the `?timeScale=` test override read by the boot scene into `ViewerOverrides` through `parseTimeScale` (finite and positive, clamped to `MAX_TIME_SCALE` 10000 so one frame can never loop through unbounded days), times the player's time speed, so the two multiply) to the state's time of day, `state.timeOfDayS`, and runs `advanceDayAllTrees(state, 1)` once for each whole `SECONDS_PER_DAY` taken off, carrying the rest; while `state.paused` the time of day stands still. A day is 900 s, 15 real minutes at the base scale: the prototype's Step_0 used 300 s, and the game uses 900 by the owner's decision. The world calls it every frame the viewer is closed, modal panels or not (the prototype stopped only on `game_paused`). **Time of day** (pure `src/sim/timeOfDay.ts`) is game state where the prototype's accumulator was a transient global: seconds into the day in [0, `SECONDS_PER_DAY`), 0 being morning, where a new game starts, every sleep ends and the day turns over, so a day still advances the world exactly once. It is saved (version 7), so a load resumes at the time it was saved and travels with the state across rooms. `timeOfDay(seconds)` gives the fraction of the day and its phase (`DAY_PHASE_STARTS`): `dawn` from 0 s, `day` from 60, `dusk` from 600, `night` from 690 until the next morning. The **time speed** (`TIME_SPEEDS` 1, 2, 4 in `src/sim/timeOfDay.ts`; `Settings.timeSpeed`, the Settings panel's `Time speed: 1x|2x|4x` row, control `panel-timespeed`, per machine and not in the save) only scales how fast real time moves the time of day and the day; a 4x day takes 225 real seconds. The HUD's time line (`timeText`, `TIME_TEXT`, under the carry line) shows the phase and, above 1x, the speed; the hook publishes `timeLine`, `timeOfDay`, `phase` and `timeSpeed`. Time of day is visual and informational only: growth, watering, revenue and the season depend on the day alone. A passing day redraws the tree sprites (growth changes their size), rebuilds an open inspector, refreshes the HUD badge and republishes; after the tick it asks `seasonTipFor(state, previousDay)` (pure, `src/sim/seasonTip.ts`) for the first-dormancy tip, which is due when the day crossed into a new season, a tree the player owns (not sold) is dormant in it (`seasonGrowthMultiplier(species.seasonsActive, season) <= 0`; that is the juniper and the maple in winter) and `state.seasonTipShown` is false: the scene then sets the flag (saved, so the tip fires once a game) and holds the text for the corner panel; display revenue above 0 shows as the toast `Display revenue: +$N` (the prototype only debug-printed it). The debug keys F1 (skip 7 days on tree 0; the inspector's `Skip 7d` does it) and F2 (advance the world 7 days) are not ported: every shipped key needs a tap control, and `?timeScale=` lets the browser tests see days pass. **Sleep** (Z, or the menu's Sleep; Sleep a week for seven) is the clock run on demand to the next morning: pure `sleep(state, days)` in `src/world/sleep.ts` runs `advanceDayAllTrees(state, 1)` once per day (so each day ticks at its own season and pays that day's display revenue, as the clock would) and sets the time of day to morning (0), however far into the day it began, charges nothing for itself and does nothing while `state.paused`; the scene allows it only while `roomKeysLive()` (no viewer, no modal) and runs the same post-tick work as the clock (`daysPassed`), toasting `Day N`, or `Day N  |  Display revenue: +$M` when the display paid. Neither the prototype's clock tick nor Sleep advances a tutorial step; the inspector's fertiliser-paid `Skip 7d` is unchanged.
- **Source plants** (obj_source_plant; the rule in pure `src/sim/sourcePlant.ts`): three 24x24 props centred in the site plan's bed along the garden's south fence below the shed, `juniper` (80, 472), `maple` (112, 472) and `pine` (144, 472) (the prototype's instance positions predate the garden's shape in the site plan), each drawn as its species' frame of `spr_source_plant` (unlabelled, as in the prototype), prompt `Take Cutting`. `takeCutting(state, plant, speciesKey)` returns the toast, with the prototype's strings: `This plant is recovering - ready on day N` while `regrowDay` is ahead; `<Display name> can't be grown from cuttings - try seeds` for a species without `cutting` in its `propagation` (maple, pine); `Cuttings are taken in spring or summer - try again on day N` outside `CUTTING_SEASONS`, N the first day of the next spring (`nextCuttingSeasonDay`); else one `cutting_<species>` into the inventory, `tutorialAdvanceIf(state, TUTORIAL_TAKE_CUTTING)`, and `Took a <Display name> cutting  (N in inventory)` (two spaces, as in the GML), the third cutting adding ` - plant now recovering` and setting `regrowDay` 14 days ahead. ### How the viewer is shaped

- A separate WebGL canvas (`#viewer`) sits fixed over the Phaser canvas and is shown only while the viewer is open.
- The camera is plain yaw/pitch/distance data in the pure `camera.ts`; the world is z-up, as in the prototype.
- Handedness: the prototype's look-at was left-handed, so the viewer mirrors the world's y axis (`toViewerSpace` in `camera.ts`, a y-flipped mesh group in `TreeViewer.ts`). Screen-right at yaw 45 is world (-1,-1,0), as in the prototype. Hotspot projection goes through the same helper.
- Rendering is on demand (one frame per open, resize or input change), never a loop, without MSAA, so a still view is a deterministic screenshot.
- Pure array builders (`potGeometry.ts`) feed Three.js `BufferGeometry`, so mesh shape is unit-tested in Node. `threeCamera.ts` uses only Three's math classes and is unit-tested too; only `TreeViewer.ts` needs WebGL.
- The tree mesh comes from the pure `treeMesh.ts`, which reads shape only from `trunkFrames`, `branchPoint` and the `*FrameAt` lookups, and colours the trunk and branches in the species' `look.trunkBark` and `look.branchBark` (`speciesBark`; the prototype's one bark pair, rgb(90, 60, 40) and rgb(110, 75, 50), is the juniper's; the maple's is smooth grey, the pine's dark). `TreeViewer.ts` keeps it as one `Mesh` in the mirrored group beside the pot and rebuilds it before a render only when `tree.revision` has changed since the last build.
- Wire is part of the bark mesh, as in the prototype: right after a wired branch's bark, `treeMesh.ts` appends an ink-brown wire helix around the branch and a 1.5-turn anchor wrap around the trunk at its base, ending on the branch's side. Every sample takes its frame from `branchFrameAt` or `trunkFrameAt`, so the coil follows the bent branch and the anchor a leaning trunk, and each tube ring is an oriented ring perpendicular to the helix tangent. Each ring's basis is the previous ring's carried along the helix (`transportBasis`), so stitched rings line up point for point and the wire never twists or pinches. Gauge and pitch scale with branch girth and bend. The coil's centre line sits `wireCoilOffset` from the branch's: the bark radius `branchRadiusAt` (the taper floored at `BRANCH_MIN_RADIUS`, the same radius the bark rings use) plus the wire radius, so the wire lies on the bark all the way to a thin branch's tip and never enters it.
- Foliage comes from the pure `foliageMesh.ts`: a seeded cluster of crossed, textured quads at each branch tip above 30% of trunk height, tinted by the species' seasonal colour through the vertex colour. The species' `look` shapes the leaves: each upright quad is `leafAspect * s` half-wide and `s` half-tall (1 is the prototype's square; the maple's 1.6 gives broad leaves, the pine's 0.35 needle tufts), `s` is the prototype's half-size times `leafSizeScale`, and a cluster holds `floor(leafCount * leafCountScale)` leaves; the juniper's look is all 1, so its foliage is the prototype's byte for byte. Every species shares the one foliage sprite. The seed is `tree.id * 1000 + branch.id` (mulberry32 in `seededRandom.ts`), so rebuilds never reroll the leaves. `TreeViewer.ts` draws it as a second `Mesh` with its own material (sprite map, alpha test 0.5, drawn from both sides).
- The season is an input, never derived: `open(tree, season, state)` and `setSeason(season)`. The viewer reads the game state's day only for wire-age captions and prune mode's `Prune: requires winter` note, never for the displayed season: the prune gate follows the game's day even when `?season=` overrides what is drawn. Foliage rebuilds before a render when `tree.revision` or the season differs from what it was built with; bark rebuilds on `revision` only.
- Colour space: the renderer outputs `LinearSRGBColorSpace` so palette bytes pass through untouched, and the four textures (foliage, bark, ground and backdrop) load with `NoColorSpace` for the same reason, filtered smoothly (the first three through mipmaps). All four are bound together once every one has loaded; `foliageReady` on the test hook says when.
- Inked: the pure builders give every vertex an outward normal (and the bark mirrored-repeat UVs), `TreeViewer.ts` indexes each mesh in both windings so it draws from both sides with those normals, lights it through `MeshToonMaterial` with the three-band ramp in the pure `toon.ts` over the Inked surround, outlines bark and pot with an ink hull pushed out a fixed 2 px in screen space and set back behind its form, and textures bark and foliage with `bark.png` and the cut-out `foliage.png`, both drawn in Inked by `npm run art:viewer` (`art/inked/viewer.mjs`). The light is the rooms' one light (`roomLight` in `toon.ts`, from `CAST_SHADOW` in `src/world/lighting.ts`): at the default orbit a shadow falls on the ground the way it falls on a room's floor, right and toward the viewer, half a unit per unit of height. The pot stands on a round wooden slab at its base (the cut-out `ground.png`, with the pot's ground shadow drawn in it away from the light, laid out by the pure `ground.ts`; unlit, drawn first and writing no depth, so it hides nothing), and behind everything a screen-space quad tiles `backdrop.png`, the surround page with its paper grain, a texel to a CSS px, tinted so its mean is the surround; the renderer clears to the surround should the textures not load. No shadow maps.
- Interaction state is plain data in the pure `hotspots.ts`: mode (`view`, `wire`, `clip`, `prune`, keys V/W/C/P), wire action, bend direction and the wired/unwired filters, reset on every `open`. `hotspotWorldPoints(tree, ui)` gives the hotspots for that state in world space (branch ones at 70% along each branch, trunk ones at 20-80% of the trunk in wire + bend) with the prototype's filters, labels and colours. Nothing in it mutates the tree.
- Hotspots are a DOM overlay (`#viewer-hotspots`, one `button.hotspot` each), not drawn in WebGL: tests read their positions from the hook and the DOM, they get hover and focus for free, and `e2e/screenshot.css` hides them so baselines stay the WebGL frame. `TreeViewer.ts` lays them out again after every rendered frame, so they follow the camera. Each button's hit circle is the prototype's 16 px radius. The overlay sits above the canvas, so a press on a hotspot never starts a camera drag.
- Clicking a hotspot acts through `src/sim/training.ts`: clip mode calls `clipBranch(tree, state, id, 1)` in any season, prune mode `pruneBranch`, which refuses unless `pruneCheck` is `ok` (winter only, `PRUNE_SEASONS`; the hotspots stay and the help line reads `Prune: requires winter`, as an out-of-stock wire's click is refused and the help says `(out)`), wire + bend `applyWire` with the targets from `branchBendTarget` (one 15 degree step on `bend` for left/right or `bendV` for up/down, its sign from `branchBendSign` with the hotspots' own projection), and a trunk hotspot `wireTrunk` at `t * trunk height` toward `trunkBendWorldAngle(bendDir, yaw)`. Wire + remove opens the confirmation (`#viewer-modal`: the pure `modal.ts` holds the prototype's sizes, colours and strings, `ViewerModal.ts` draws it); its Remove calls `removeWire`. While it is open its dim takes every pointer event, the toolbar and hotspots are inert, Escape closes the modal (not the viewer) and other keys do nothing. Hotspot clicks are ignored while a frame is pending, so a second click before the buttons are rebuilt cannot hit a branch a prune just renumbered. Apart from `state.paused` (below), the viewer never writes the tree or state itself: the sim functions do, consuming wire from the inventory and refusing when there is none (the help line then reads `(out)`); a successful action bumps `tree.revision`, so the next frame rebuilds the mesh and the toolbar.
- The toolbar is DOM too: `#viewer-toolbar` (tree info, View/Wire/Clip/Prune and Exit buttons, and in wire mode the action, bend-direction and show sub-row) and `#viewer-help` (the bottom help lines, with the wire stock in wire mode and, in prune mode outside winter, `Prune: requires winter` above the camera line). The pure `toolbar.ts` builds a plain-data model from the tree, interaction state, wire stock and day, holds the prototype's sizes, colours and strings, and gives the wire-age caption under wired hotspots; `ViewerToolbar.ts` draws it after every frame (skipping an unchanged model) and reports clicks to `TreeViewer`'s setters and `close()`. `open(tree, season, state)` takes the `GameState` so it can read the wire stock and day and pass it to the training functions. Mode and camera keys ignore Ctrl, Meta and Alt chords; Escape closes the viewer unless the confirmation is open.
- The viewer pauses the simulation, as the prototype did with `global.game_paused`: `open` sets `state.paused = true` (only once WebGL is available) and `close` (Escape, the Exit button, or the owner) sets it back to false on the state it was opened with. `advanceDayAllTrees` is a no-op while paused. The flag is transient: `serializeState` never writes it and every load starts unpaused. `paused` on the test hook mirrors it.
- `projection.ts` maps a world point to CSS px with Three's math classes only (unit-tested in Node). It goes through `toViewerSpace`, as the handedness rule demands, and drops points behind the camera or outside the near/far range.
- The top strip (60 px, 110 px in wire mode, the prototype's toolbar and wire sub-row) never starts a drag; on a phone the DOM toolbar wraps taller and takes the press itself. Wheel zoom works anywhere over the canvas; pinch zoom wherever the first finger could start a drag; the camera buttons wherever they are drawn.
- Pinch zoom: `pinchZoom(camAtPinchStart, startPx, nowPx)` in `camera.ts` moves `distance` by the change in finger distance over `PINCH_PX_PER_NOTCH` (20 px) wheel steps, continuously, clamped to the wheel's limits, and never touches yaw, pitch or target. `TreeViewer.ts` tracks the pointers down on the canvas: one starts a drag (below the top strip); a second landing during the drag ends it and starts a pinch from the camera at that moment; moves during the pinch only zoom; lifting either finger ends the pinch, and the finger left down needs a new press to drag. A third finger is ignored. Opening the confirmation or closing the viewer cancels a drag or pinch.
- Camera buttons (`#viewer-camera`, built once by `ViewerToolbar.ts`): zoom in (`+`), zoom out (`−`) and reset (`R`), a column at the right edge, vertically centred, sized by `CAMERA_BUTTON_SIZE` (36), `CAMERA_BUTTON_GAP` (8) and `CAMERA_BUTTONS_RIGHT` (20) in `toolbar.ts`. Zoom is one wheel step (`zoomCamera` by -1 or +1), reset is `resetCamera`, as the wheel and R. Inert with the toolbar under the confirmation; hidden by `e2e/screenshot.css` so baselines stay the WebGL frame.
- On a viewport narrower than `SUBROW_ONE_LINE_WIDTH` (830 px, the bend sub-row on one line at the prototype's 24 px group separator) the sub-row's groups sit `SUBROW_SEPARATOR_NARROW` (12 px) apart (`subRowSeparator(viewportWidth)` in `toolbar.ts`), so a phone in landscape (824 px wide) keeps the wire toolbar at 110 px instead of wrapping over the top of the tree. Exit sits in the mode buttons' flex flow (16 px after Prune), so on a narrow viewport it wraps beside Prune rather than taking a row of its own. Branch hotspots stack above trunk hotspots, so where the two overlap on a short viewport a tap takes the branch. The help lines and the modal are unchanged at every size; the S22 Ultra in landscape (`s22-landscape`) shows the tree and the whole pot between them in every mode.
- The direction helpers `trunkBendWorldAngle` (screen direction to a world bend angle at the current yaw) and `branchBendSign` (which bend sign moves a branch tip the chosen way on screen, found by projecting a perturbed copy) are pure and take the projector as an argument; the wire actions use them.
- Screenshot tests open fixture trees (`viewerFixtures.ts`, grown through `src/sim` actions) with a `?tree=<cutting|bent|cascade|maple|wired|fancy>` URL parameter, and choose the viewer's season with `?season=<spring|summer|autumn|winter>` (default: the game's season), both read by the boot scene; the tests start a new game from the title with Enter and open the viewer with V in the world.
- Screenshot baselines live in `e2e/__screenshots__/` and are regenerated on Linux with `npx playwright test --update-snapshots=all`.

## Invariants carried over

These are the prototype's hard-won rules, each with where it lives in the web project.

- **The tree model is the single source of truth.** 2D sprite and 3D mesh both derive from it. No display state stored separately from morphology. (`src/sim/tree.ts`)
- **Real units.** Trunk height in cm, girth in mm. Scaling for display happens in the viewer only. (`src/sim/`, `src/viewer/`)
- **Scoring is derived, never persisted.** Recompute on demand from morphology, style and vitality. (`src/sim/scoring.ts`)
- **Species and styles are data.** Keyed records, looked up by key; trees store the key. A species' look (bark, sprite trunk, leaf shape) is data on it too, never code per species. (`src/sim/species.ts`, `src/sim/styles.ts`)
- **Seasons are a pure function of the game day.** 28-day seasons, year starts in spring on day 1, nothing persisted. A new game starts on day 0 as the prototype did; `seasonForDay` treats it as day 1. The saved time of day never moves the season. (`src/sim/seasons.ts`)
- **All growth goes through the season multipliers.** `seasonGrowthMultiplier` returns 0 for dormancy; any path that adds height or girth must consult it. Fertilising refuses when it is 0. Season also gates the non-growth paths: repotting is spring-only, taking a cutting is limited to `CUTTING_SEASONS` (spring and summer) and taking seeds to `SEED_SEASONS` (autumn), and pruning (removing a branch) to `PRUNE_SEASONS` (winter) through `pruneCheck`, while clipping stays year-round. (`src/sim/seasons.ts`, `src/sim/growth.ts`, `src/sim/sourcePlant.ts`, `src/sim/training.ts`)
- **Trunk and branch shape come from one curve helper each**, shared by the mesh builder, hotspot UI and style scorers, so what you see is what you score. (`src/sim/geometry.ts`)
- **Mesh rebuilds are triggered by a revision counter** bumped by every morphology mutation. (`tree.revision`, read by `src/viewer/`)
- **Consumables are really consumed.** Wire, fertiliser and pots are decremented by the action that uses them, gated on availability. (`src/sim/inventory.ts`)
- **Fertiliser is timed.** The item is consumed; the effect lives on the tree until a day threshold. (`src/sim/growth.ts`)
- **Location is a tagged string**, not an enum: a room name, `inventory`, `sold`, or `displayed:<pedestal-key>`. (`src/sim/tree.ts`)
- **Tutorial state is one step counter** advanced idempotently from gameplay call sites. (`src/sim/tutorial.ts`)
- **The 3D viewer pauses the simulation.** `state.paused` (the prototype's `global.game_paused`) is true while the viewer is open; the day advance does nothing while it is. Transient, never saved. (`src/sim/world.ts`, `src/viewer/TreeViewer.ts`)
- **Saves carry a version** and old shapes migrate forward. Prototype saves import one way. (`src/sim/save.ts`)

## Build, test, deploy

- **Gates** (`npm run check`, mirrored in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`): ESLint strict type-checked, Prettier, `tsc --noEmit`, Vitest with coverage, Vite build, then Playwright against the built output. The whole suite runs in two profiles, `chromium` (desktop Chrome, 1280 x 720) and `s22-landscape` (Drew's Samsung Galaxy S22 Ultra in Chrome at its default FHD+ display mode, 824 x 384 CSS px at scale factor 2.8125), with the parity loop in `s22-landscape-parity` after them. Browser tests whose time is real-time walking on software GL (the long door-to-door routes and the 3D world's walking tests) carry the Playwright tag `@walk`: the PR check runs with `--grep-invert @walk`, and the scheduled `walk-tests.yml` workflow runs only `@walk` tests on `main`. The parity loop is its project's only test, so it stays untagged and in the PR check. The two phone-portrait profiles, `mobile` (Pixel 7) and `s22-portrait` (384 x 824), run only `e2e/orientation.spec.ts`, because a phone held upright gets the rotate prompt and nothing else: that spec checks the prompt and the pause under it there, and on desktop that turning 384 x 824 to 824 x 384 at runtime refits the world canvas and re-renders the viewer without a reload.
- **Tap parity.** The browser tests drive the game through `e2e/drive.ts`. On the S22 projects (`tapRoute: true`) it reaches every action through its visible control by touch; elsewhere through its key or a click. So the suite proves tap parity on the phone and keeps the shortcuts tested on desktop. Gestures (drags, pinch) are the same everywhere; movement keys and the few tests of key handling itself stay keys on every project.
- **Build version.** `vite.config.ts` defines `__APP_VERSION__` as `yyyy.mm.dd.build` through the pure `src/buildVersion.ts`: the UTC date of the commit being built (`git log -1 --format=%cI`) and `GITHUB_RUN_NUMBER` (`0` locally), so it depends only on the commit and the CI run, never on when the build ran; `package.json`'s semver stays the package's own. `__APP_COMMIT__` is `GITHUB_SHA` or `git rev-parse HEAD` (`unknown` without either, with the date `0000.00.00`), and `__APP_COMMIT_URL__` its GitHub page, which the title's version line opens in a new tab.
- **PWA.** `vite-plugin-pwa` generates the manifest and a Workbox service worker that precaches the build. The precache size cap is raised because Phaser alone exceeds the default. With `registerType: 'autoUpdate'`, an updated worker's reload goes through `onNeedReload` (`src/main.ts`), and `src/platform/swUpdate.ts` saves the running world's active slot before the page reloads; on the title it reloads at once.
- **Hosting.** Azure Static Web Apps. `public/staticwebapp.config.json` sets SPA fallback, security headers and cache policy. Hashed chunks under `/assets/` are immutable for a year, while the fixed-name sprite sheets (`/assets/spr_*`) and sounds (`/assets/audio/*`), regenerated in place by the art scripts, are `no-cache` so a browser revalidates them; the routes are matched in order, first match wins, and `src/platform/csp.test.ts` pins the order. The Content-Security-Policy is one of those headers, and `index.html` carries the same policy as a `<meta http-equiv>` (less `frame-ancestors`, which browsers ignore there), so `vite dev`, `vite preview` and therefore the whole browser suite run under it; `src/platform/csp.test.ts` pins the two equal, and `e2e/csp.spec.ts` fails on any CSP violation in the console. Everything loads from the game's own origin; Phaser's built-in textures need `data:` images and the inline `<style>` in `index.html` needs `style-src 'unsafe-inline'`. Strict-Transport-Security asks browsers for HTTPS for a year, subdomains included, without `preload`, because joining the browsers' preload lists is the domain owner's decision; the same unit test pins it. `.github/workflows/deploy-swa.yml` deploys `main` to production and each PR to a preview environment, and removes the preview on close. The deploy token lives in a repository secret; without it the workflow verifies the build and skips.
- **Security.** Gitleaks on every push and PR; CodeQL via the repository's default setup; Dependabot for npm and Actions, grouped weekly.

## Known rough edges

- The shed is drawn in Inked at the scale rule's heights, with its decor (shelf, tools, watering can, pot stack, sack; the floor clutter y-sorted with the player), the garden from the prototype's sprites, and the house rooms (new content: the lounge-kitchen, the hallway and the study) in Inked with their furniture, their doors from placeholder sprites, the display spots on the lounge's mantel and side table and the study's desk and bookshelf; the shed has no display spots. The shed is the prototype's inset room, framed by the dark background on a desktop and filling the view on a phone, where the world camera zooms in. The title starts a real new game or continues a save, the days pass in real time, you can walk around and between the rooms, save with F5, carry trees between the floor and the display pedestals, take juniper cuttings (and, in autumn, seeds of every species) in the garden and plant them at the planting table, and V opens the fully interactive 3D viewer on the first tree in the state (or a `?tree=` fixture). The shop kiosk sells supplies and the workbench makes pots from clay.
- Not ported from the prototype: the title's 3D hero tree (`scr_title_hero`), the debug keys F1 and F2, the window `scale` setting and Quit. Prototype saves import through `src/sim/save.ts`, but the game has no control to bring a `saveN.json` file in.
- The prototype's rough edge that only junipers grow from cuttings carries over; every species grows from seed.
